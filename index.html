Step 1: Core Concepts

Glossary & Real-World Analogies

Understand the fundamental building blocks of neural networks through intuitive real-world mental models.

🧠

The Neuron

Like a biological brain cell, an artificial neuron receives input values (x), multiplies them by weights (w), adds a bias (b), and passes the result through an activation function.

Analogy: A tiny decision maker in a committee.
⚖️

Weights (w)

Weights determine the importance or strength of each incoming signal. Higher weight means the input has more influence on the final output decision.

Analogy: How much you trust a friend's advice.
🎯

Bias (b)

Bias is an offset that shifts the activation threshold. It decides how easily a neuron fires regardless of the incoming inputs.

Analogy: A person's natural predisposition or mood.

🧠 Core Concepts Before You Train

Learning Rate (α): Think of this as your step size when walking down a foggy mountain. If your step size is too big, you overshoot the valley and bounce back and forth. If it is too microscopic, it takes forever to reach the bottom. A rate around 0.15 is a healthy, balanced stride.

Convergence: This simply means reaching the destination. When the error (loss) stops decreasing and flattens out, the AI has successfully learned the pattern and 'converged' to the best possible solution.
